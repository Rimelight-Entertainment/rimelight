# The Ohrn

The Ohrn is a cosmogonic relic of insurmountable power. It is the tournament's "artefact of attraction." Its sturdy metal body and elegant curves guide the design of most of the game's visual elements, from architecture to interfaces.

Its magic is divine in nature. It appears as golden beams of energy, representing the vital energy that flows through every living being. Its sound can be heard across the universe.

**Inscription:**

> "While you live, sound - have all grief be through, for the end exists avowed and such demands its due."

The inscription is inspired by the [One Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Ring). It was written based on the [Seikilos Epitaph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seikilos_epitaph), considered the oldest complete musical composition ever found. The Grand Tale theme motif is also derived from it.

**Also known as:** the Cradle. Reduced form: a necklace.

## Name

The name is based on the Kabbalistic concept of the [Ohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohr). It is also a pun on the misspelling of "horn," and it relates to the [shofar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shofar).

## Trivia

- The idea of a horn in the game comes from the original Defense of the Ancients custom map for Warcraft III, where a horn sounds at the start of a match. The same element was kept in its sequel, Dota 2.
- The Arhus is partly inspired by Heimdall from Norse mythology. Heimdall blew the Gjallarhorn to warn the gods of the start of Ragnarok, just as the Arhus is sounded at the start of a match.
- "Gjallarhorn" may mean "horn of the river Gjöll." In Norse myth, Gjöll is the river that separates the living from the dead, much as the Arhus has power over life.
- Horns were also historically used as drinking vessels. Heimdall drinks from Mímisbrunnr to gain wisdom. Blowing the Arhus is akin to drinking the waters of life.
- "Arhus" comes from the river Aarhus in Jutland, Denmark, and roughly means "river mouth."
- Related ideas: fountain of youth, water of life, olifant / oliphant (instrument).
