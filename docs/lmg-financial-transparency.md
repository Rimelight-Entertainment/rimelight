# LMG Financial Transparency — Analysis & Notes

**Status:** Reference / research notes (for further consideration, not policy)
**Topic:** How Linus Media Group (LMG) allocates its expenses, based on the financial transparency data shared in their video.

## Overview

A breakdown of how **Linus Media Group (LMG)** spends its money based on the financial transparency data shared in the video.

---

## 1. Overall Expense Breakdown (% of Total Company Expenses)

Combining **inventory purchases** and **payroll** makes up **50% of LMG's total expenses**.

| Category                          | % of Total Expenses | Specific Breakdown / Notes                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Inventory Purchases**           | **26%**             | • Screwdrivers & warehouse stock: **14%**<br />• Backpacks & gear items: **8%**<br />• Apparel: **4%**                     |
| **People / Payroll**              | **~25%**            | Covers a headcount of approximately 120 full-time employees.                                                               |
| **Product Fulfillment & Storage** | **10%**             | Storage and distribution channels (includes **8%** specifically paid to couriers/shipping).                                |
| **Taxes**                         | **~8%**             | Corporate tax installments.                                                                                                |
| **Utilities**                     | **~3%**             | Building utilities under overhead.                                                                                         |
| **Selling Costs**                 | **3%**              | Shopify platform fees and payment processors (PayPal, Stripe).                                                             |

_Overhead also covers items like company events, insurance, security, janitorial services, R&D, and charitable contributions._

## 2. Payroll Budget Breakdown

Payroll accounts for roughly **25% of total expenses** across ~120 employees. The internal breakdown of where payroll dollars go includes:

- **Internal Shared Services:**
  - **Operations:** **>11% of payroll** (includes top executive salaries, HR, community management, and company events).
  - **Accounting, IT Infrastructure, Logistics:** Comprise the remainder of shared services.
- **Developers:** Teams building Floatplane, LTTStore, LTT Labs, and internal tools.
- **Writing:** **10%–15% of payroll** (scriptwriting, video planning, research, hosting).
- **Production:** **10%–15% of payroll** (editors and camera operators).
- **Product Design:** **10%–15% of payroll** (engineering, graphic design, fashion/apparel).
- **LTTStore Operations & Management:** **10%–15% of payroll** (customer care, compliance, supply chain, marketing).
- **LTT Labs Team:** **<10% of payroll** (product benchmarking and testing).
- **Partnerships & Procurement:** **<10% of payroll** (brand sponsorships and purchasing).

---

## 3. Salaries & Compensation

- **Median Salary:** Increased from **~$73,500 CAD** in 2024 to **>$80,000 CAD** in 2025 (a 9% increase tethered to the Greater Vancouver living wage).
- **Average Salary:** **~$114,000 CAD** in 2024 and **~$115,000 CAD** in 2025.
- **Executive Compensation:**
  - CEO (Terren) to median worker pay ratio is between **5:1 and 10:1**.
  - Founders Linus and Yvonne take a base salary of **$121,000 CAD each**, taking additional earnings via shareholder dividends from net profit.

---

## 4. Where the Net Profit (~15%) Goes

The remaining **~15% net profit** is used for:

1. **Reinvestment:** Funding upcoming content projects (such as renovating a "tech house" and major 2026 content acquisitions).
2. **Cash Reserves:** Maintaining an operational rainy-day fund (which prevented layoffs during COVID and the 2023 controversy).
3. **Owner Dividends:** Shareholder payouts to the owners.

---

## Key Takeaways (Complementary Analysis)

- **Two dominant cost centers.** Inventory (26%) + payroll (25%) are half the business. Everything else is sub-10%. Any meaningful margin work has to target sourcing/warehouse efficiency or headcount productivity — there is no third lever hiding in the budget.
- **Payroll is the big controllable lever.** ~120 employees at a ~$115k CAD average ≈ a high seven-figure CAD annual payroll. A company of Rimelight's scale should track payroll as % of revenue separately from content spend.
- **Net profit ≈ operating discipline.** The ~15% is split between reinvestment, rainy-day reserves, and owner dividends. The rainy-day fund explicitly absorbed COVID and the 2023 controversy — a strong argument for keeping cash reserves ahead of aggressive hiring.
- **Interesting benchmark — rent at only 2%.** Building loans are held by separate holding companies with rent paid to them for tax/liability reasons. That's a deliberate structural choice, not an accident, and worth remembering when comparing "rent" numbers across companies.
- **Compensation philosophy.** Median salary is explicitly tethered to the Greater Vancouver living wage and rose ~9% YoY. Executive ratio (5:1 to 10:1) is modest; founders take modest salaries and earn via dividends (i.e., tied to net profit, not fixed cost).
- **Where Rimelight differs.** LMG is product-heavy (inventory is their #1 line item). A content/entertainment company without physical merch would see payroll dominate far more (>40%) while logistics-related lines (10%) nearly vanish — so these percentages should not be copied, only the _methodology_ and _philosophy_.

## Open Questions

- What split between reinvestment, reserves, and owner dividends is the right default for Rimelight as it scales?
- Should compensation be benchmarked to a regional living wage, or to the industry (tech/media) market rate?
- How should juxtaposed cost structures (pure content vs. product/merch) inform where we invest first?
